### The Problem

Imagine humanity has exhausted Earth's resources and the clock is ticking to find a new home among the stars. You are tasked with analyzing thousands of distant, blurry pixels to decide where millions of people will live. You must design a self-sustaining habitat in a hostile environment, figure out how to autonomously mine extraterrestrial resources without human intervention, and ensure the entire structure won't collapse under extreme cosmic stresses. The survival of the colony depends on getting the math exactly right before the first ship even launches.

### Overview & Impact

Competing against 26,000 students worldwide in the National Space Society (NSS) Space Settlement Contest, our team **placed 3rd globally** and was invited to present our work at the prestigious **International Space Development Conference**.

### Technical Architecture & Simulation

Our space settlement design integrated machine learning, 3D modeling, and structural engineering physics:
- **Exoplanet Machine Learning:** I developed a machine learning model trained on a dataset of over 5,000 exoplanets from NASA to identify and rank the optimal exoplanet candidates for future human colonization.
- **Autonomous Robotics Simulation:** I created highly detailed drone models using **Blender** and simulated their physics and control logic in **Gazebo** to demonstrate autonomous resource collection and mining operations on an extraterrestrial surface.
- **Structural Engineering (FEA):** I designed the overarching settlement structure in Blender and conducted rigorous Finite Element Analysis (FEA) and structural simulations in **SimScale** to ensure the habitat could withstand environmental stresses.

### Challenges

Running complex Finite Element Analysis (FEA) on large-scale settlement structures in SimScale pushed the limits of our computational resources. Furthermore, training the exoplanet ML model required careful handling of imbalanced datasets, as habitable exoplanets make up a tiny fraction of the NASA Kepler data.

### Next Steps

Future research will involve integrating atmospheric spectroscopic data into the exoplanet model to improve habitability predictions beyond just physical planetary dimensions.
