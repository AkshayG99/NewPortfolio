### The Problem

Imagine a bright high school student who is fascinated by technology but feels completely disconnected from traditional computer science classes. They learn abstract theory on a whiteboard but never get to see their code come to life in the real world. Without hands-on experience building real apps or wiring actual circuits, their passion fizzles out before they even reach university. There is a massive gap between the outdated school curriculum and the thrilling, hands-on reality of modern software and hardware engineering.

### Overview & Impact

I led district-wide STEM initiatives, successfully teaching programming and engineering concepts to over **60 students**. The goal was to bridge the gap between theoretical knowledge and practical, hands-on application in computer science and hardware engineering.

### Curriculum & Workshops

- **Software Engineering:** I designed a comprehensive development curriculum where students were guided to build real-world applications. The coursework covered modern frameworks and tools including **React, Flask, TensorFlow, and AWS**, using **Python, JavaScript, and C++**.
- **Hardware & Embedded Systems:** Beyond software, I taught students the fundamentals of **Arduino (C++)**, circuit design, and embedded systems, including PCB basics, enabling them to create their own physical hardware devices.
- **Project-Based Learning:** I delivered numerous interactive, project-based workshops focused on coding, circuit building, and the critical integration between hardware and software systems.

### Challenges

Teaching complex topics like React, TensorFlow, and C++ to high school students with diverse technical backgrounds was challenging. I had to rapidly iterate on the curriculum to ensure it remained engaging without being overwhelmingly difficult, especially when debugging physical hardware (Arduino).

### Next Steps

I plan to open-source the curriculum and project blueprints so that other school districts can easily adopt and scale the program.
